    What it does

    Four questions, one board

    Before a season fails, a county disaster officer needs four answers. Ukame Watch gives each one per county, in plain language.

    Is this season unusual?

    Rainfall as % of the 1991–2020 normal, and an NDMA-style drought level for every county.

    What comes next?

    A 3-month outlook tested against two baselines, and a live 7-day heavy-rain and heat watch.

    Who is exposed?

    Census population per county, and the rise in hot days since 1991.

    What do we prepare?

    A cited action playbook, and an offline AI that drafts an advisory a named officer must approve.

    Proof, not promises

    It catches the disasters Kenya already lived through

    10 of 10

    documented droughts and floods, 1997–2024, flagged by the indicators

    6 of 6

    worst droughts and heavy-rain periods in 35 years of data match a documented disaster

    +0.12

    forecast skill for Oct–Dec rains on unseen years 2017–2026 (Brier skill score against climatology)

    +5 to +10

    extra hot days per decade, statistically significant in 7 of 8 counties

    Where it fails: no skill for the March–May long rains (−0.05). The board says so on screen, and every limit is published.

    Track rules

    Built the way the rules ask

    Open data, free tools

    Every source, access date and licence is recorded in DATA.md. No paid services.

    The AI never calculates

    Code computes every number; each one in an advisory must match a tool result, or the draft is flagged.

    Every tool call logged

    Tool calls, answers and human decisions are kept in audit logs.

    “I don’t know” when data is missing

    Unknown counties, future dates and cyclone paths are refused instead of guessed.

    A person approves

    Advisories stay drafts until a named officer approves them.

    No personal data

    Only public, aggregated data. The AI runs locally, so nothing is sent to a hosted service.

    Honest results

    No forecast skill for the March–May rains, and we say so. Data errors are logged, not hidden.

    Models cited, AI disclosed

    Qwen 2.5 3B (local, Qwen Research License), logistic regression, and Claude Code as coding assistant: see the README.

    Sustainable Development Goals

    Earlier action on Kenya’s costliest climate hazards

    13.1Climate Action

    County drought, heavy-rain and heat signals before a season fails.

    2.4Zero Hunger

    Time for water repairs, pasture planning and timely livestock offtake.

    1.5No Poverty

    Protects household assets in some of Kenya’s poorest counties.

    11.5Sustainable Communities

    Heavy-rain watches prompt early warning and evacuation plans.

    What we can claim: Ukame Watch shortens the time between a rainfall signal and a county conversation about preparedness. It does not measure hunger or losses, and it has not yet been tested with county officers.

    Live

    The live board

    The full dashboard, running in your browser with live data and the 7-day forecast. It started loading when you opened this page.

    Inside the board, the county cards and hazard map come first. Below them, seven tabs give the detail for the county you pick under County in focus:

    1. Season historyEvery rainy season since 1991 as % of normal, with documented disasters labelled
    2. Seasonal outlookThe chance of a very dry next 3 months, and whether the model beat both baselines
    3. 7-day watchThe live forecast against the county’s own heavy-rain and heat extremes
    4. Heat stressHot days per year since 1991, with trend tests for every county
    5. Track recordThe 10 documented disasters it caught, and the cross-check against ERA5
    6. Draft an advisoryThe AI assistant, with approve or reject. It runs live on a laptop; online it shows the recorded test run
    7. Method & limitsThe full limitations note
